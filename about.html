About Rukiyah Polk · Money Behavior Strategist

I got tired of money advice that assumes knowing better means doing better.

People are constantly told to budget, save more, stop spending, build credit, and make better money decisions.

But what happens when you already know that — and you still keep doing the same thing?

That question is a big part of why I became a Money Behavior Strategist™.

I’m a money educator, but I don’t just teach people what they should do with money. I want to know what happens between getting paid and wondering where the money went.

My Story

I had to learn how to make my money last, too.

There was a time when I was broke and homeless. I was a mom of six who had slept in shelters and depended on other people just to get by.

Later, I had income coming in, but I still had to learn what to do with it before I spent it.

In 2009, I divided my income into four parts: 70% to LIVE, 10% to SAVE, 10% to GROW, and 10% to GIVE.

That meant all the money in my account was not there to spend. Each part had a job.

That changed how I used my money. It also made me pay attention to what I thought about money and what I did when I got paid.

The Work I Do Today

I didn’t arrive here from one direction.

I bring together money education, financial health counseling, credit, trauma-informed money work, behavior change, and lived experience.

But I don’t use that training to hand you another list of things you already know you “should” be doing.

I use it to help you figure out what happens between getting paid and wondering where the money went.

That work became Money Behavior Strategy™: we look at what you did with the money, why it happened, what you can try instead, and what your next check needs to do before you spend it.

When You and I Work Together

We start with what actually happened to your money.

Maybe you planned to save $100 and spent it.

Maybe payday came Friday and by Monday you’re already checking your balance before buying something.

Maybe you make more money than you used to, but somehow there still isn’t much left.

That’s where I work.

We look at what came in, what had to be paid, where the rest went, and what was happening when you made those choices.

Then we decide what needs to happen differently the next time money hits your account.

We don’t stop at “make a budget.” We make a plan you can actually use when you get paid.

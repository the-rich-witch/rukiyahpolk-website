My Story

I had to learn how to make my money last, too.

There was a time when I was broke and homeless. I was a mom of six who had slept in shelters and depended on other people just to get by.

Later, I had income coming in, but I still had to learn what to do with it before I spent it.

In 2009, I divided my income into four parts: 70% to LIVE, 10% to SAVE, 10% to GROW, and 10% to GIVE.

That meant all the money in my account was not there to spend. Each part had a job.

That changed how I used my money. It also made me pay attention to what I thought about money and what I did when I got paid.

The Work I Do Today

My own money lessons became part of the work I do now.

I went on to study money, behavior, trauma, credit, and the way our thoughts and life experiences can affect what we do with money.

I bring those pieces together in Money Behavior Strategy™.

You don’t have to know how to make a budget before you come to me. You don’t have to know how much to save. You don’t have to have your money figured out first.

When You and I Work Together

We start with the money you’ve and what’s happening right now.

You show me how much money comes in and when you get paid.

You show me the bills that have to be paid, what you spend on food and everyday needs, what you spend on things you want, and what’s left.

Then we find the point where the money starts getting low.

We talk about what was happening when you spent it. Were you buying something you needed? Treating yourself? Helping someone? Spending because you were stressed? Telling yourself you would save on the next check?

Those thoughts about money are part of your money mindset. They can affect what you do when money comes in.

Once we can see what’s happening, we make a plan for your next check. We decide what gets paid, what you need for everyday life, what you can enjoy, what you’ll save, and what needs to stay there for later.

Your money gets a job before you start spending it.

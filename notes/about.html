About

I've never been able to pick just one thing.

And after years of trying to make that a problem to solve, I stopped. This is the long version of how a Money Behavior Strategist, a nonprofit founder, and a rootworker turned out to be the same person.

The founder story

I've been teaching the money method that became 70/10/10/10 since 2009, back when it was just how I rebuilt my own finances after mine fell apart. There was no framework yet — just a stubborn habit of asking "okay, but what actually happened here," instead of accepting the version of events that made everyone feel better.

That question turned out to be useful for a lot more than money. It's the same question underneath Now Alternative Soulutions, and it's the same question I bring to a cartomancy reading. I'm not interested in the story that's supposed to be true. I'm interested in the one that is.

Money Behavior Strategy™

I'm a Money Behavior Strategist and money educator, and the founder of Money Behavior Strategy™. My work brings together money education, financial health counseling, trauma-informed money work, and behavior change — because knowing what you should do with money doesn't always mean you'll actually do it. That gap is the whole job.

Now Alternative Soulutions

I founded Now Alternative Soulutions as a 501(c)(3) nonprofit serving Kern County, California, and I serve as its Executive Director. NAS runs four community programs — Periods Still Happen, Community Care Connect, Kidz Thrive 360, and Where's The Funds? — putting period products, hygiene essentials, family support, and financial education directly into people's hands. Dignity, empowerment, and access, not paperwork and not pity.

Rootwork and practice

My rootwork practice predates either business by more than a decade. I'm a lineage-trained rootworker, a community-centered herbalist, and a cartomancer working with traditional playing cards rather than tarot. This tradition runs on its own logic, its own lineage, and its own respect for the work — it doesn't get folded into the money-behavior framework, and the money-behavior framework doesn't get folded into it.

Philosophy

Every part of what I do rests on the same premise: people aren't broken, and their circumstances usually make more sense than they've been told. Shame doesn't change behavior — clarity does. So whether we're looking at a bank statement or a spread of cards, the job is the same: see what's actually there, understand why it's there, and figure out what to do next.

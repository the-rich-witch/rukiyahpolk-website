What community work actually looks like, once you take the photo op out of it

On running a nonprofit in the county you grew up in, and why "awareness" isn't a program.

People ask what Now Alternative Soulutions does, and I've learned to answer with a specific example instead of a mission statement, because mission statements are where good intentions go to stop being useful. Here's the honest version: a family needs something concrete this week, and we try to be one of the places that can actually provide it, without making them perform gratitude or explain themselves first.

That's not glamorous. It doesn't photograph well. It's also the entire job.

Awareness isn't a program

I've sat in a lot of rooms where "raising awareness" got treated as an accomplishment on its own. Awareness is not nothing, but it isn't a program, and it definitely isn't access. You can be extremely aware that a family needs support and still have built nothing that reaches them on a Tuesday afternoon when the need is actually happening.

We built NAS's programs around that gap on purpose — direct, specific, and aimed at the moment someone needs something, not the moment someone needs to feel informed.

Doing this where you're from

Running a nonprofit in Kern County means the people you serve are sometimes people you know, or people who know someone you know. That's not a complication to manage — it's the whole point. Community work done by an outsider looking in tends to solve the problem the outsider can see. Community work done by someone from here tends to solve the problem that's actually there.
